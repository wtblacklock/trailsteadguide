## 2. Fixed

### Wyoming state parks do still have first-come camping (1 guide, 4 places)

**The finding.** `camping-in-wyoming-for-beginners` asserted a blanket, year-round reservation
requirement for Wyoming state park camping, in four places, and drew the explicit conclusion
that drive-up camping is gone:

| Line | Surface | Was |
|---|---|---|
| 70 | `faqPageGraph` JSON-LD | "Yes. Wyoming State Parks moved to a reservation requirement for camping, so the old plan of driving up and finding a first-come site at a state park **no longer works**" |
| 94 | `QuickAnswer` summary | "Note that Wyoming state park camping **now requires a reservation** at reserve.wyoming.gov" |
| 175 | Body, "Wyoming state parks" | "camping at Wyoming state parks **now requires a reservation**, so the drive-up-and-hope approach **no longer applies**" |
| 313 | Visible FAQ | "Yes. Camping **now requires a reservation** through reserve.wyoming.gov, plus a park pass" |

**What is actually true.** Wyoming State Parks runs a *seasonal* reservation requirement, not a
universal one:

- Outside each park's reservation season, the year-round campgrounds are **entirely first come,
  first served**. The official reservations page puts that window at roughly October 1 through
  April 30.
- **Even during the reservation season, most parks keep first-come "walk-in" sites.** The state's
  own reservations page says so explicitly and publishes a separate annual list of them.
- Season dates **vary by park**: Boysen, Glendo, Guernsey, Keyhole and Seminoe run mid-May to
  mid-September; Buffalo Bill, Medicine Lodge and Sinks Canyon run May through September;
  Curt Gowdy runs mid-May through September 30.

**Why this one mattered more than most.** The guide recommends **Curt Gowdy, Glendo and Sinks
Canyon** by name as the right places for a first Wyoming trip, in the same breath as telling the
reader that driving up is pointless. Those are precisely the parks where the claim fails: Curt
Gowdy is first come, first served from October 1 to May 14 and keeps Federal Bay and Hecla Point
first-come all year; Glendo is first come, first served from September 15 to May 15. A reader
planning a shoulder-season or winter trip to the parks this guide recommends was being told the
one thing that would stop them going.

**What the fix adds.** All four surfaces now state the seasonal split, name the walk-in sites,
and give the season dates by park group. The edit also adds two specifics the guide did not have
at all: the booking window is **120 days ahead for Wyoming residents and 113 days for
non-residents**, and new inventory releases **daily at 7:00 a.m. Mountain** (a 2026 change from
the previous 1:00 a.m. release, with cancelled sites now held and released together the
following morning). Page and structured data agree. **No outbound link was edited.** The fix
adds specificity rather than retreating from it, as the brief requires.

**Corroboration.** Four independent searches returned a consistent account across multiple
distinct official pages (the statewide reservations page, and the Curt Gowdy, Glendo and Boysen
park pages), plus independent third-party roundups. Where sources conflicted they were resolved
toward the official page and the conflict is noted rather than averaged: one third-party guide
claims a 1:00 a.m. Mountain release and another claims ReserveAmerica is the booking system,
both contradicted by the state's own pages, and neither was used.

- [Wyoming State Parks - Reservations](https://wyoparks.wyo.gov/index.php/permits-reservations/reservations)
- [Wyoming State Parks - Curt Gowdy reservations](https://wyoparks.wyo.gov/index.php/reservations-curt-gowdy)
- [Wyoming State Parks - Glendo reservations](https://wyoparks.wyo.gov/index.php/reservations-glendo)
- [reserve.wyoming.gov - Plan Your Visit](https://reserve.wyoming.gov/web/Activities/PlanYourVisit.aspx)

**What was deliberately not asserted.** Per-park walk-in site *counts* change annually and are
published as a separate document that could not be opened from this sandbox, so the guide says
most parks keep "a handful" rather than giving numbers. Reservation fees ($4 resident / $8
non-resident) and the 18-day modification lockout on maximum-window bookings were corroborated
but left out as too granular for a beginner guide. The 14-day-in-30 stay limit was likewise left
out; it is real but belongs in a different section than the one being corrected.

### Three standing corrections to the brief, all previously flagged and still unactioned

1. **The proxy status port is not `127.0.0.1:36667`.** It is assigned per session: `38711` on
   2026-10-05, `42409` on 2026-10-19, `35327` on 2026-10-26, **`38779`** this run. Use
   `curl -sS "$HTTPS_PROXY/__agentproxy/status"`. Flagged four times now.
2. **`validate:affiliate` does not emit `[403]` lines.** It produced **zero** `[403]` and 142
   `[error: fetch failed]`, summarising as `0 OK, 0 not buyable, 142 broken, 0 unverified`.
   It exited **1** this run, where it exited **0** on 2026-10-26, so the exit code is not stable
   either. Anyone matching on `[403]` to recognise the known-noise case will miss it and may read
   "142 broken" as a real finding. **It is not.**
3. **The trip-pack known-noise item is obsolete and should be deleted from the brief.** The suite
   is a clean **570/570**; `lib/pdf/render.ts` resolves a browser from `PLAYWRIGHT_BROWSERS_PATH`,
   which exists here. The brief still describes this as an expected failure, which is a standing
   licence to ship a red test. Flagged on 2026-10-05, 2026-10-12, 2026-10-19 and 2026-10-26.

A fourth, new this run: **the brief's grep guidance is itself locale-fragile.** The recommended
`grep -P '\x{2014}|\x{2013}'` failed on this container with

the same pattern worked with `-rl` across directories. The dash sweep was therefore confirmed
with a short Python scan over `app/`, `components/`, `lib/` and `data/`, which is locale-proof
and is what the 0 result in section 1 rests on.
