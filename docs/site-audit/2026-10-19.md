### `camping-in-colorado-for-beginners` never mentions RMNP timed entry - carried over, now seasonally dormant

Unchanged from 2026-10-12 and still not auto-fixed, but the timing has shifted in a way that
matters for *when* someone should pick it up.

Rocky Mountain National Park ran a timed-entry permit system for the 2026 season, separate from
the entrance pass and booked on Recreation.gov. The Colorado guide has a dedicated section
calling RMNP "the headliner - and the hardest reservation in the state", names four of its
campgrounds, and tells beginners how far ahead to book - and says nothing about timed entry. A
reader could hold a valid campground reservation and still be turned away at the gate.

Re-confirmed this run: the 2026 system ran **May 22 to October 12** (general park) and
**May 22 to October 18** (Bear Lake Road corridor), released on the 1st of each month at 8 a.m.
MDT with next-day releases at 7 p.m. MDT ([NPS 2026 news
release](https://www.nps.gov/romo/learn/news/rocky-mountain-national-park-announces-2026-timed-entry-reservation-system.htm);
[Visit Estes Park](https://www.visitestespark.com/rocky-mountain-national-park/timed-entry-permit-reservation/)).

**Both windows have now closed** - the Bear Lake corridor's last day was October 18, yesterday.
No 2027 dates are announced, and the park has announced the following year's system in February
in each recent year. So right now the omission is harmless: there is no live system to miss.
It becomes harmful again the moment 2027 is announced.

That gives a clear window rather than an open-ended backlog item: **write this copy between now
and the 2027 announcement**, in wording that does not hardcode dates - roughly "RMNP requires a
summer timed-entry permit in addition to your campground reservation; check nps.gov/romo for
this year's dates". Phrasing and placement across the body, QuickAnswer, FAQ and JSON-LD is a
content judgment, so it stays out of an audit sweep. Still **recommendation 2**.

Confirmed structural again this run: `timed entry` and `entrance reservation` appear in only
`camping-in-utah-for-beginners` across `app/` and `lib/`. Glacier's system is covered in the
Montana guide under different wording. Of the three park-entry systems the location guides
touch, two are covered and Colorado's is absent.

**The stargazing guide's astronomy is correct, and its nearest claim goes live in two days.**
`stargazing-camping-with-kids` is the only guide on the site making falsifiable astronomical
predictions, and it makes six. All six check out:

- **Orionids** - guide says "peak around October 21 and 22, 2026, with roughly 10 to 20 meteors
  an hour under dark skies; the moon sets after midnight, so the best viewing is in the early
  morning hours." Confirmed: peak the night of October 21-22 (RASC predicts 18 UTC October 21),
  15-25/hr under dark skies, and a waxing gibbous moon ~83% lit that sets after midnight
  (03:12 from New York), making the post-moonset pre-dawn hours best
  ([EarthSky](https://earthsky.org/clusters-nebulae-galaxies/everything-you-need-to-know-orionid-meteor-shower/);
  [Astronomy](https://www.astronomy.com/the-sky-this-week/the-sky-this-week-from-october-16-to-23-2026/)).
  The rate range is appropriately conservative and the moon logic is right.
- **Leonids** - "peak around November 17 and are usually modest, around 10 to 15 an hour."
  Confirmed: November 16-18, a moon-friendly year, and modest non-storm rates
  ([Almanac meteor calendar](https://www.almanac.com/content/meteor-shower-calendar)).
- **Geminids** - "around December 13 and 14 are the strongest shower of the year and the best one
  for kids, because they are active by mid-evening and the moon is a thin crescent that sets
  early in 2026." Confirmed on every element: peak December 13-14, strongest annual shower,
  radiant up by mid-evening, and a thin waxing crescent ~21-23% lit that sets in the evening
  ([Farmers' Almanac](https://www.farmersalmanac.com/geminid-meteor-shower);
  [StarWalk](https://starwalk.space/en/news/geminid-meteor-shower)).

This is worth recording as a positive precedent: a guide that commits to specific, checkable,
expiring numbers and gets all of them right, including a moon-phase inference. It also means
these three claims will need re-checking in the 2027 content pass - they are the most reliably
perishable facts on the site.

## 4. Blocked by sandbox

**Blocked is not clean.** No claim is made in either direction about anything in this section.

Every outbound host this audit needs is refused by the egress proxy. Confirmed once by direct
probe, then not retried: `www.nps.gov`, `www.recreation.gov`, `images.unsplash.com` and
`www.amazon.com` all return curl code `000`, and the proxy's own `recentRelayFailures` log
reports `connect_rejected` / `gateway answered 403 to CONNECT (policy denial)`. Even
`www.google.com` is denied, which confirms the policy is a blanket third-party deny rather than
a per-host list. `WebSearch` works and was the sole research tool for section 7.

**Two corrections to the standing brief, both about this section:**

1. The brief hardcodes the proxy status endpoint at `127.0.0.1:36667`. The port is assigned per
   session: it was `38711` on 2026-10-05 and **`42409`** this run. Use
   `curl -sS "$HTTPS_PROXY/__agentproxy/status"`. (Flagged on 2026-10-05; still in the brief.)
2. The brief says `validate:affiliate` fails with "hundreds of `[403]` lines". **That signature
   has changed.** This run produced **zero** `[403]` lines and 130 `[error: fetch failed]` lines
   instead, summarising as `0 OK, 0 not buyable, 130 broken, 0 unverified`. The cause is
   identical - the proxy refuses CONNECT, so the fetch never completes - but anyone matching on
   `[403]` to recognise the known-noise case will now miss it and may read "130 broken" as a real
   finding. **It is not.** The brief's wording should be loosened to cover both signatures.

Consequences:

- **548 distinct external URLs unchecked** - 226 Amazon/`amzn.to` affiliate links, 16 nps.gov, 69
  YouTube embeds, the remainder agency/reservation portals and reference citations. **Nothing was
  edited or removed on the strength of a failed request.**
- **238 distinct image URLs unchecked** - 115 `m.media-amazon.com`, 112 Unsplash, 5 Wikimedia, 4
  `images-na.ssl-images-amazon.com`, plus 2 `example.test` fixtures in tests. **No image URL was
  changed**, and no photo ID was invented.
- **`npm run validate:affiliate` exits 1 reporting 130 broken links.** These are proxy CONNECT
  denials, not Amazon responses. The script cannot tell the two apart, so **this run says nothing
  about affiliate-link health in either direction.** Do not read it as broken; do not read it as
  verified.

To restore these three checks, the environment's network policy needs `nps.gov`,
`recreation.gov`, the state park agency domains, `images.unsplash.com`, `amazon.com` and
`m.media-amazon.com` allow-listed. Until then this audit is repo-only plus WebSearch. This is
the fifth consecutive run blocked the same way; it is a standing environment limitation, not a
transient failure.
