### 2.1 Wyoming state parks do not require reservations the way the guide said (1 guide, 5 places)

**The finding.** `camping-in-wyoming-for-beginners` asserted a blanket reservation requirement:

| Line | Surface | Was |
|---|---|---|
| 70 | `faqPageGraph` JSON-LD | "Yes. Wyoming State Parks moved to a reservation requirement for camping, so the old plan of driving up and finding a first-come site at a state park no longer works." |
| 94 | `QuickAnswer` summary | "Note that Wyoming state park camping now requires a reservation at reserve.wyoming.gov." |
| 175 | Body, state-parks section | "camping at Wyoming state parks now requires a reservation, so the drive-up-and-hope approach no longer applies" |
| 238 | Common-mistakes list | "Assuming you can grab a first-come state park site. Wyoming state parks now require camping reservations" |
| 313 | Visible FAQ | "Yes. Camping now requires a reservation through reserve.wyoming.gov" |

**What is actually true.** Wyoming State Parks runs a **per-park reservation season**, roughly
mid-May to mid or late September depending on the park (Boysen, Glendo, Guernsey, Keyhole and
Seminoe May 15 to September 15; Curt Gowdy May 15 to September 30; Buffalo Bill, Medicine Lodge
and Sinks Canyon May 1 to September 30). During that season most campsites must be reserved. But
**a limited number of first-come, first-served walk-in sites are retained at most parks** - the
agency maintains and publishes a current FCFS site list - and **outside the reservation season
every site is first-come**.

All five surfaces now state the season structure, keep the walk-in caveat, and keep the practical
warning the guide was reaching for (treat a popular summer weekend as reservation-only and book
it). Page and structured data agree.

**Why this mattered enough to fix rather than flag.** The guide's claim is wrong in both
directions and wrong *right now*. Today is November 2: every Wyoming state park is outside its
reservation season, so the site is currently telling readers they must book something that cannot
be booked and is free to walk into. In summer it errs the other way, telling readers that walk-in
sites do not exist when the agency publishes a list of them. The error also sat in the
common-mistakes list, which is the one place a reader is most likely to trust as a hard rule.

**Corroboration.** Two independent searches both returned the official
`wyoparks.wyo.gov` reservations page as the authoritative account, consistently on all three
points (per-park season dates, retained walk-in sites, off-season FCFS). Third-party aggregators
that disagreed were older (Campendium and Roadtrippers both cite a superseded May 1 to September
30 season) and were not treated as evidence. **No outbound link was edited** - `reserve.wyoming.gov`
and `wyoparks.wyo.gov` are unchanged.

**What was deliberately not asserted.** The reservation-opening window. One third-party source
gives 120 days for residents and 113 for non-residents, and another gives a 7:00 a.m. release for
freed-up sites, but neither was corroborated and the guide's existing "check the current details
on the reservation site" pointer covers it. The edit adds specificity about the season structure
and declines to add a number it could not verify twice.

- [Wyoming State Parks - Reservations](https://wyoparks.wyo.gov/index.php/permits-reservations/reservations)
- [Wyoming State Parks - Curt Gowdy camping](https://wyoparks.wyo.gov/index.php/activities-amenities-curt-gowdy/camping-tents-curt-gowdy)
- [reserve.wyoming.gov - plan your visit](https://reserve.wyoming.gov/Web/Activities/PlanYourVisit.aspx)

### 2.2 A guide shipped this cycle gave out-of-state readers Florida's resident window (1 guide, 4 places)

for a national audience deciding where to take a winter-break trip, and its explicit premise is
that "families head to Florida, the Gulf Coast, south Texas, southern Arizona, or the southern

reservation page, the Florida Senate's own bill analysis for CS/HB 109, WUSF's reporting when the

the 2026-09-03 correction, which still holds exactly.

**Cross-checked site-wide.** Every other place the site states Florida's window now carries the
split: `camping-in-florida-for-beginners` line 180 states it in full, and
`best-state-parks-for-families` line 156 states it in full. The 2026-10-26 report flagged the
latter as scoped-and-therefore-acceptable; it has since been upgraded to the explicit split, so

**Texas, in the same list, was checked and is correct.** "About five months ahead" matches the
official ReserveAmerica park pages (5 months, 8 a.m. Central), with documented per-park

### 2.3 Literal `&apos;` was reaching FAQPage structured data (5 guides, 12 instances)

**The finding.** `lib/seo.ts:270` passes each FAQ item's `a` string straight into the JSON-LD
`acceptedAnswer.text` field. Five location guides had `&apos;` written inside those JS string
literals, where - unlike JSX text - nothing decodes it. Verified in the built output rather than
inferred:

```
.next/server/app/guides/camping-in-the-appalachians-for-beginners.html
  "acceptedAnswer":{"@type":"Answer","text":"Variable. The Great Smoky Mountains&apos; popular campgrounds ...
```

Twelve instances across `camping-in-the-appalachians-for-beginners` (5),
`camping-in-the-northeast-for-beginners` (3), `camping-in-the-desert-southwest-for-beginners` (2),
`camping-in-florida-for-beginners` (1) and `camping-in-the-pacific-northwest-for-beginners` (1).

**The fix.** Each replaced with the repo's existing convention for these strings, a backslash
escape (`\'`), which the surrounding guides already use - `how-to-choose-a-family-campsite` and
`camp-stove-fuel-in-cold-weather` both do it this way. The rendered JSON-LD now carries a real
apostrophe.

**Why it counts as a defect rather than cosmetics.** This is the text search engines parse for
FAQ rich results, and the visible FAQ on the same pages renders correctly, so page and structured
data disagreed. It is also mechanically verifiable end to end, which is the class the brief says
to auto-fix. The same scan found entities inside JS strings in `app/api/*/route.ts`,
`lib/pdf/template.ts`, `lib/signup-alert.ts` and `lib/email-templates.ts`; all are deliberate
HTML-escaping maps or HTML email bodies and were **not** touched.

### 3.2 Six audits have reported "0 dashes" from a grep that could not match any dash

**This corrects a number in every report since 2026-09-03, including this one's first draft.**

The brief warns that a naive `grep '[—–]'` triggers locale range collation and produces false
positives, and prescribes matching the literal code points instead (`grep -P '\x{2014}|\x{2013}'`).
That prescription is correct but incomplete: in this sandbox `LANG` and `LC_ALL` are both **unset**,
so PCRE runs without UTF-8 mode and `\x{2014}` silently matches **nothing at all**. The failure is
the opposite of the one the brief describes - not false positives, but a guaranteed zero.

```
$ grep -roP '\x{2014}|\x{2013}' app/ components/ lib/ data/ | wc -l
0
$ LC_ALL=C.UTF-8 grep -roP '\x{2014}|\x{2013}' app/ components/ lib/ data/ | wc -l
267
```

Re-run correctly, the true picture is still clean where it matters:

| Directory | Occurrences |
|---|---|
| `app/` | 0 |
| `components/` | 0 |
| `lib/` | 0 |
| `data/` | **267** |

All 267 are in two files, and **neither is site content**: `data/affiliate-coverage.csv` (262) and
`data/research/recommendations.csv` (5). Nothing in the repo reads either one - there is no
`readFileSync` of a `.csv`, no CSV parser dependency, and no reference to them anywhere under
`app/`, `lib/`, `components/` or `scripts/`. They are internal working spreadsheets, so the
`CLAUDE.md` rule, which is scoped to "site content (guide/compare/plan copy, JSX text, metadata,
JSON-LD strings)", does not reach them.

**Not fixed, deliberately.** `data/affiliate-coverage.csv` is explicitly off-limits per the brief,
and it holds 262 of the 267. The other five are in a research working file where the dashes sit in
prose descriptions ("Squeeze bottle with built-in flip bowl - water + bowl in one"). Editing it
changes nothing a reader sees and touches a file a human workflow writes to.

**The real risk, and why this is worth recording rather than shrugging off:** those
`recommendations.csv` rows are product blurbs, which is exactly the kind of text that gets pasted
into a guide later. If that happens, the dashes come with it. The standing fix is to add the
correct check to CI next to the metadata guard from #131, so this stops depending on whichever
shell the audit happens to run in. **Recommendation 4.**

So: section 5 is **clean for site content**, which is what the previous six reports were trying to
say. But they could not have known that, and the next audit should not trust a bare `grep -P` in
this sandbox without `LC_ALL` set.
