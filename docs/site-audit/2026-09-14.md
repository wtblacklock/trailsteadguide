## 2. Fixed

### Factual corrections (3 guides)

**1. Acadia's release window was wrong, and the page contradicted itself.**
`app/guides/camping-in-the-northeast-for-beginners/page.tsx` said in its common-mistakes list that
"Blackwoods and Seawall release 60 days ahead and book in seconds". The same page states the
correct rule in five other places (FAQ answer, JSON-LD FAQ, QuickAnswer summary, the reservation
bullet list, and the federal-lands body paragraph), all of them "6 months out". The 60-day number
matches nothing.

Corroborated from two independent directions:

- NPS's own announcement: beginning December 1, 2024, **90% of Acadia campground reservations are
  available up to six months ahead; the remaining 10% are released two weeks ahead**, including
  same-day ([Acadia National Park updates availability of campground
  reservations](https://www.nps.gov/acad/learn/news/20241125.htm)).
- The Recreation.gov campground pages for Blackwoods and Seawall independently state the same
  split: 90% six months in advance on the first of the month at 10am EST, the last 10% at 10am
  14 days ahead on a rolling basis
  ([Blackwoods](https://www.recreation.gov/camping/campgrounds/232508),
  [Seawall](https://www.recreation.gov/camping/campgrounds/234674)).

The fix keeps the mistake item's point and makes it sharper rather than vaguer: two months out is
now identified as *exactly* the dead zone between the 6-month bulk release and the 2-week
remainder. The five correct statements elsewhere on the page needed no change, so page and
structured data now agree.

**2. Labor Day guide still carried the "7 a.m. local time" error.**
`app/guides/labor-day-weekend-camping/page.tsx` said federal campgrounds release "at 7 a.m. local
time exactly six months out". This is the precise error the 2026-09-03 audit identified and fixed
in `recreation-gov-reservation-strategy` (six places) and `camping-in-colorado-for-beginners` (two
places): the Recreation.gov drop is a **single nationwide moment**, so "local time" is wrong for
everyone outside the Pacific zone. That sweep missed this occurrence.

Changed to "7 a.m. Pacific (10 a.m. Eastern)", matching the canonical phrasing in
`recreation-gov-reservation-strategy`. Re-confirmed this run that the underlying rule is unchanged:
6-month rolling window, 7am PT / 8am MT / 10am ET
([Recreation.gov reservation
tips](https://www.recreation.gov/articles/list/tips-for-making-campsite-reservations-at-popular-locations/281),
corroborated by [Campnab's booking
guide](https://campnab.com/blog/your-ultimate-guide-to-booking-a-campsite-on-recreation-gov)).
The rest of that page already said "6-month rolling window" without a time and needed no change.

**3. Florida entry in the state-parks roundup predated HB 109.**
`app/guides/best-state-parks-for-families/page.tsx` told readers to book Myakka River "11 months in
advance for winter weekends". Since January 1, 2024 that window is **residents only**; non-residents
get 10 months ([CS/HB 109 staff
analysis](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2023/109/Analyses/h0109z1.ACR.PDF),
[Florida State Parks: Early Access to Camping for Florida
Residents](https://www.floridastateparks.org/early-access-camping-florida-residents)). This is also
an internal-consistency fix: `camping-in-florida-for-beginners` has stated the split correctly since
the 2026-09-03 audit, so the roundup was contradicting the state guide it links to.

Changed to "11 months in advance for Florida residents, 10 months for everyone else", with the
peak-season note kept as its own sentence.

## 4. Blocked by sandbox

**Blocked is not clean.** No claim is made in either direction about anything in this section.

Every outbound host this audit needs is refused by the session's egress proxy with
`gateway answered 403 to CONNECT (policy denial)`. Confirmed this run by direct probe:
`www.nps.gov`, `www.recreation.gov`, `images.unsplash.com`, `www.amazon.com` all return curl code
`000`, and all four appear in the proxy's own `recentRelayFailures` log as `connect_rejected`.
`WebFetch` returns `EGRESS_BLOCKED` for the same hosts. `WebSearch` works and was the sole research
tool for section 7.

Consequences:

- **406 distinct external URLs unchecked.** 62 agency/reference links across **46 distinct hosts**
  (nps.gov x11, recreation.gov, reddit x5, and one each for 43 state park and agency domains),
  72 Amazon/`amzn.to` affiliate links, 42 YouTube embeds, the remainder self-referential, schema.org
  or test fixtures. **Nothing was edited or removed on the strength of a failed request.**
- **203 distinct image URLs unchecked** - 91 Unsplash, 76 `m.media-amazon.com`, 30 Wikimedia, 4
  `images-na.ssl-images-amazon.com`. **No image URL was changed**, and no photo ID was invented.
- **`npm run validate:affiliate` is unusable.** It exits 1 with **156 `[403]` lines and nothing
  else** - every single link checked, no other status code anywhere in the output. These are proxy
  CONNECT denials, not Amazon responses; `www.amazon.com:443` is in the proxy's rejection log. The
  script cannot tell the two apart, so **this run says nothing about affiliate-link health in
  either direction.** Do not read it as broken; do not read it as verified.

Partial mitigation used instead of fetching: every agency domain the site links to appeared as a
live, current URL in this run's WebSearch results, including `nps.gov`, `recreation.gov`,
`dnr.wisconsin.gov`, `midnrreservations.com`, `reserve.wyoming.gov`, `wyoparks.wyo.gov`,
`parks.wa.gov`, `nhstateparks.org`, `dec.ny.gov`, `floridastateparks.org` and the ReserveAmerica
state subdomains. That is evidence against a silent domain rename. It is **not** evidence that any
individual deep link still resolves.

To restore these three checks, the environment's network policy needs `nps.gov`, `recreation.gov`,
the state park agency domains, `images.unsplash.com` and `amazon.com` allow-listed. Until then this
audit is repo-only plus WebSearch.

### Process notes for the next run

- **The brief's proxy-status URL is wrong for this container.** It gives
  `http://127.0.0.1:36667/__agentproxy/status`; the proxy was on **41701** this run and the port is
  not stable between sessions. Use `curl -sS "$HTTPS_PROXY/__agentproxy/status"` instead, which
  works regardless.
- **The dash grep caveat still applies** and the Python/Node codepoint scan is still the right
  tool. `grep -P '\x{2014}'` errors out in this container's non-UTF-8 locale and, with stderr

  `components/`, `lib/`, `data/`; that is what the 0-violation result is based on.
- **Grep for the constant's *use*, not its name.** The 15-page compare miss happened because three
  audits searched for `META_TITLE`. Resolving what each `pageMetadata()` call actually passes is
  the check that holds up. Worth keeping as the standing method.

## 6. Gap recommendations

Ranked. Item 1 has now been the top item for three consecutive audits and has gotten a second,
independent piece of evidence this week.

1. **Build the canonical reservation-systems reference table.** *(S effort, high lift)* Third
   consecutive audit fixing booking-window errors caused by 19 guides each independently restating
   the same external facts. Week 1: three mutually inconsistent Recreation.gov release times.
   Week 2: California generalising Yosemite's exception, Utah citing a nonexistent 12-month window.
   Week 3 (this run): Acadia's 60-day claim contradicting the same page five times over, and a
   surviving "7 a.m. local time" the week-1 sweep missed. Note the shared shape of all three: the
   wrong number is never in the FAQ or QuickAnswer, it is buried in a body list where nobody
   re-reads it. One sourced table (state, system, URL, window, release time) that the guides link
   to instead of restating is both a citable asset and the structural fix. **Ship this before the
   next state guide.**
2. **Enforce the metadata budgets in the type system.** *(S effort, high lift - new this week)*
   The compare-page miss is the same disease as item 1: 115 hand-rolled `pageMetadata()` call sites,
   no two agreeing on what to name the constant, and no check. Two options, either is fine: a
   branded-type or builder that rejects an over-length title/description at compile time, or a
   vitest case that walks the catalogue and asserts the budgets. The test is the cheaper one and
   would have caught all 17 of this week's fixes plus the 21 flagged in section 3. **This is the
   single highest-value item on the list that is not item 1.**
3. **Split `metaDescription` out of `Printable` (and consider the same for `Skill`).**
   *(S effort, medium lift)* 10 of 15 printable descriptions are over the SERP limit and cannot be
   trimmed because the field doubles as visible card copy. `Guide` already separates the two; making
   `Printable` match is a small schema change that unblocks 10 metadata fixes at once. See
   section 3.
4. **Guide: "What NOT to bring camping".** *(backlog Tier 1 #6, S effort)* Still the only unshipped
   Tier 1 content item and still the cheapest remaining win. Unchanged for three weeks.
5. **Decide what `seasonal` is for.** *(S effort, editorial)* Three season-shaped guides in a row
   have now been filed elsewhere. Either recategorise (gear storage, Halloween and after-dark are
   all defensible seasonal entries, which would take the category from 4 to 7 with no new writing)
   or accept that seasonal means only the four broad-season guides and stop calling it a gap.
6. **Second wave of state guides: WA, OR, NC, TN.** *(M effort each)* Region guides already link
   these agencies without a destination. Do this *after* items 1 and 2, so the four new guides
   consume the table and the metadata check rather than adding four more copies of the problem.
7. **Gear bundles to match the catalogue.** *(backlog Tier 2 #12, S-M effort)* 98 products against
   4 bundles. Budget and mid tiers are the natural cuts.
8. **Location index / state picker.** *(S effort)* 18 location guides with no browse surface between
   the hub and the article.
9. **Upgrade the New York booking window** to the specific NYSDEC 9-month window and release times
   - see flagged. The sourcing is done and recorded in section 3; it is a natural by-product of
   building item 1.

Editorial items deliberately kept out of the commit: all new content above, the seasonal
recategorisation, the New York upgrade, the printables and skills title/description trims, and any
image replacement.
