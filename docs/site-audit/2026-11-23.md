## 2. Fixed

### 2.1 Wyoming's state-park reservation requirement is seasonal, not year-round (1 guide, 5 places)

**The finding.** `camping-in-wyoming-for-beginners` told readers, without qualification, that
Wyoming state parks require a camping reservation and that "the drive-up-and-hope approach no
longer applies." The requirement is in fact bounded to the summer reservation season. Outside it
the parks revert to first-come, first-served and the system **will not take a booking at all**,
which is the opposite of what the guide implied.

The guide also stated no booking window, hedging instead with "release times and rules have
changed in recent seasons." The window is specific and published: **120 days for residents, 113
for non-residents.**

| Line | Surface | Was |
|---|---|---|
| 70 | `faqPageGraph` JSON-LD | "Yes. Wyoming State Parks moved to a reservation requirement ... the old plan ... no longer works" |
| 94 | `QuickAnswer` summary | "Wyoming state park camping now requires a reservation at reserve.wyoming.gov" |
| 175 | Body, "Wyoming state parks" | "camping at Wyoming state parks now requires a reservation, so the drive-up-and-hope approach no longer applies" |
| 238 | Mistakes list | "Wyoming state parks now require camping reservations through reserve.wyoming.gov" |
| 313 | Visible FAQ | "Yes. Camping now requires a reservation through reserve.wyoming.gov" |

All five now state the May 1 to September 30 reservation season, the 120/113-day resident and
non-resident windows, and the October-through-April first-come reversion. Page and structured
data agree.

**Why this matters now rather than in spring.** The guide's own framing is a three-month high
season with "lower-elevation state parks in the east and the Bighorn Basin" having "a longer
season on both ends." A reader acting on that today, in late November, is squarely in the
first-come window the guide told them does not exist, and would either skip a trip that is
available or try to book one that cannot be booked.

**The per-park caveat is in the edit, deliberately.** The switchover date is not uniform, and the
exceptions are parks this guide names by name. Sinks Canyon and Glendo run first-come through
April 30; **Curt Gowdy and Guernsey run it into mid-May.** The body edit names Curt Gowdy and
Guernsey specifically and points readers to the individual park page, because a reader following
this guide's first-trip recommendation is most likely headed to exactly those parks.

**Corroboration.** Four of Wyoming's own park pages (Curt Gowdy: "October 1st through May 14th,
all campsites are first come, first serve. No reservations can be made during this time frame";
Sinks Canyon, Glendo, Guernsey similarly), plus independent secondary confirmation of the
statewide season, plus the reservation platform vendor's own launch announcement for the 120/113
day windows and their $4/$8 fees.

- [Wyoming State Parks - Reservations](https://wyoparks.wyo.gov/index.php/permits-reservations/reservations)
- [Curt Gowdy reservations](https://wyoparks.wyo.gov/index.php/reservations-curt-gowdy)
- [Sinks Canyon campsites](https://wyoparks.wyo.gov/index.php/activities-amenities-sinks-canyon/campsites-for-rvs-sinks-canyon)
- [Glendo reservations](https://wyoparks.wyo.gov/index.php/reservations-glendo)
- [Roadtrippers - state park camping reservations by state](https://roadtrippers.com/magazine/state-park-camping-reservations/)
- [Tyler Technologies - Reserve.Wyoming.gov launch](https://investors.tylertech.com/news/news-details/2024/Wyoming-State-Parks-New-Reservation-Site-is-Now-Live-Powered-by-Tyler-Technologies/default.aspx)

**What was deliberately not asserted.** Guernsey's news list carries a headline, "Updated Rules
and Regulations Effective Jan. 1, 2027," which could not be opened from this sandbox. Nothing was
written about it. See section 3. One source (Hipcamp) gives 133 days for non-residents against
the official 113; the official figure was used and the outlier ignored. **No outbound link was
edited.**

### 2.2 Florida's 11-month window is residents-only, and the winter-break guide's readers are not residents (1 guide, 4 places)

**The finding.** `winter-break-camping-with-kids` stated the Florida State Parks booking window
as "up to 11 months ahead" in four places. Florida's window has been split since HB 109 took
effect on 2024-01-01: **11 months for Florida residents, 10 for everyone else.**

This is a defect *in this guide specifically*, which is why it is fixed here and was not fixed in
`best-state-parks-for-families`. The winter-break guide's whole premise is out-of-state travel:
its own copy says "many northern campgrounds are closed, so families head to Florida, the Gulf
Coast, south Texas." Its reader is, by construction, the non-resident who gets 10 months. A
reader planning from the headline number would open their booking attempt a month before the
system would accept it, in the single most oversubscribed week of Florida's camping year.

Fixed in the `faqPageGraph` JSON-LD (line 52), the `QuickAnswer` summary (83), the body booking
list (149) and the visible FAQ (314). All four now give both windows and name which applies to an
out-of-state family.

**Corroboration.** The state's own FAQ and the bill analysis, independently.

- [Florida State Parks FAQ](https://www.floridastateparks.org/plan-your-visit/faqs)
- [Early Access to Camping for Florida Residents](https://www.floridastateparks.org/early-access-camping-florida-residents)
- [CS/HB 109 analysis](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2023/109/Analyses/h0109z1.ACR.PDF)

By contrast, `best-state-parks-for-families` scopes its 11-month figure to "Florida families," so
11 is correct for its stated reader. Left alone, as in previous audits.

## 6. Gap recommendations

Ranked. The first is unblocking rather than content, as it has been for five audits, but the thing
that needs unblocking has changed.

1. **Run the publisher 20 times, or fix whatever stopped it.** *(zero authoring effort, highest
   lift)* Twenty finished guides are past their publish date, the oldest three weeks old. This
   single action ships eight state guides, the Tier-2 etiquette guide and the Tier-2 Leave No Trace
   guide, and moves the location cluster off the 20 it has held for three baselines. It replaces
   "merge the queue," which is now **done** - the 18-PR backlog that topped this list for four
   audits is gone and 50 commits landed. Worth diagnosing whether this is a stalled cron, a
   failing CI step or simply nobody running it, because 26 of 30 queued items not publishing on
   schedule is a process failure, not a content one.
2. **Build the canonical reservation-systems reference table.** *(S effort, high lift)* Fifth
   audit at or near the top of the content list, and this week it has its best evidence yet in
   two directions. The two fixes in section 2 required **nine edits for two facts**. Section 3.5
   catalogues **fourteen statements of five facts** that all go stale on the same date five weeks
   out. One sourced, dated row per system, with guides linking instead of restating, converts both
   of those into one-line edits. **Ship before the eight queued state guides publish**, or they
   will add eight more copies of the same facts.
3. **Write the RMNP timed-entry copy.** *(S effort)* No longer time-boxed against an imminent
   announcement, which makes it easier, not less necessary: nothing published for 2027, and the
