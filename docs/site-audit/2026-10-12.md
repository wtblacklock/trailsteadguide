## 2. Fixed

### Factual correction: Buffalo National River reservations (1 guide, 4 places + JSON-LD)

`camping-in-the-ozarks-for-beginners` is one of the two newest location guides, and it shipped
with a reservation fact that expired inside the same cycle.

The guide said, in four places, that Kyles Landing and Erbie "stay first-come, first-served".
That was correct when written: the park's January 2026 announcement moved five campgrounds
(Steel Creek, Ozark, Carver, Tyler Bend, Rush) to reservation-only for the 2026 season and
explicitly kept Kyles Landing, Erbie and some Buffalo Point sites first-come, first-served.

**On September 1, 2026 the National Park Service extended reservations to all individual
campsites at Kyles Landing and Erbie as well.** Corroborated across two independent search
results before editing, per the brief:

- Arkansas Outside, [Buffalo National River Expands Reservation System to Kyle's Landing and
  Erbie Campgrounds](https://arkansasoutside.com/buffalo-national-river-kyles-landing-erbie-reservations-2026/):
  beginning September 1, 2026, all individual campsites at both campgrounds require reservations
  through Recreation.gov or 877-444-6777; group sites at Erbie remain reservable; cash and checks
  are not accepted.
- A [Kyles Landing campground listing](https://campflare.com/campground/kyles-landing-307)
  independently states that Recreation.gov reservations are required starting September 1, 2026,
  with flush restrooms and potable water through November 15, 2026.

The park's own camping page is an older snapshot and still describes the pre-September state,
which is exactly why this needed two corroborating sources rather than one fetch.

All four statements were updated so page and structured data agree:

| Location | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| `page.tsx:54` JSON-LD `faqPageGraph` | "Kyles Landing, Erbie, and some sites at Buffalo Point stay first-come, first-served." | "Kyles Landing and Erbie joined them on September 1, 2026, which leaves only some sites at Buffalo Point first-come, first-served." |
| `page.tsx:109` body bullet | "Kyles Landing, Erbie, and some Buffalo Point sites stay first-come, first-served, which matters on a busy October Saturday." | "Kyles Landing and Erbie switched to reservation-only on September 1, 2026, so only some Buffalo Point sites are still first-come, first-served." |
| `page.tsx:222` common mistakes | "Book on recreation.gov, or have Kyles Landing or Erbie as a fallback and arrive early." | "Every developed campground on the river except some Buffalo Point sites switched to reservation-only during 2026, Kyles Landing and Erbie as of September 1. Book on recreation.gov before you drive out, because cell service along the river is unreliable." |
| `page.tsx:284` visible FAQ | "Kyles Landing, Erbie, and some Buffalo Point sites stay first-come, first-served." | "Kyles Landing and Erbie joined them on September 1, 2026, leaving only some Buffalo Point sites first-come, first-served." |

The common-mistakes entry mattered most and is the one that changed in substance rather than
detail. Its advice was **actively harmful after September 1**: it told readers to drive to Kyles
Landing or Erbie as a walk-up fallback, which is now a four-hour round trip to a campground that
will turn them away. The replacement keeps the mistake being described ("assuming Buffalo River
sites are walk-up") and substitutes the correct action, adding the park's own reason for booking
in advance (no reliable cell service on the river) rather than retreating to something vaguer.

The guide's other Buffalo River facts were re-verified and are correct as written: the six-month
booking window, same-day availability, no cash or checks, the confirmation-number requirement,
and the March 13 to November 15 2026 season. Missouri's 12-month state-park window, asserted in
the same guide, is also correct.

### `camping-in-colorado-for-beginners` never mentions RMNP timed entry (material omission)

Not an incorrect statement, so not auto-fixed, but the most significant accuracy gap found this
week and arguably more consequential for a reader than the Ozarks error.

Rocky Mountain National Park **required a timed entry permit for the 2026 season**: 9 a.m. to
2 p.m. daily from May 22 through October 12 for the general park, and 5 a.m. to 6 p.m. through
October 18 for the Bear Lake Road corridor, booked on Recreation.gov with a $2 fee, one per
vehicle per day, and separate from the entrance pass ([NPS timed entry permit
system](https://www.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/timed-entry-permit-system.htm);
[Visit Estes Park](https://www.visitestespark.com/rocky-mountain-national-park/timed-entry-permit-reservation/)).

The Colorado guide has a dedicated "RMNP and the popular state parks book early" section, calls
RMNP "the headliner - and the hardest reservation in the state", names four of its campgrounds,
and tells beginners how far ahead to book. It says nothing about timed entry anywhere. A reader
who followed it exactly could hold a valid campground reservation and still be turned away at
the gate.

Confirmed structural, not a one-off: `grep` for `timed entry` and `entrance reservation` across
`app/` and `lib/` returns hits in **only** `camping-in-utah-for-beginners`. Glacier's system is
covered well in the Montana guide under different wording. So of the three park-entry systems
the site's location guides touch, two are covered and Colorado's is absent.

Deliberately left out of the branch for a reason beyond "editorial": **the 2026 permit window
closed today**, October 12, and the 2027 dates are not announced. Any text written now would
either be about a system that just ended or would need to guess at 2027. The correct copy is
roughly "RMNP requires a summer timed entry permit in addition to your campground reservation;
check nps.gov/romo for this year's dates", which is a judgment call about phrasing and placement
across the body, the QuickAnswer, the FAQ and the JSON-LD. That belongs to a content pass, not an
audit sweep. **Recommendation 2 in section 6.**

## 4. Blocked by sandbox

**Blocked is not clean.** No claim is made in either direction about anything in this section.

Every outbound host this audit needs is refused by the egress proxy. Confirmed this run: the
proxy's own `recentRelayFailures` log lists `m.media-amazon.com:443` and `www.google.com:443` as
`connect_rejected` with `gateway answered 403 to CONNECT (policy denial or upstream failure)`.
`WebSearch` works and was the sole research tool for section 7.

The brief's hardcoded proxy port (`127.0.0.1:36667`) is wrong again, as the 2026-10-05 audit
warned: it was **38329** this run. Use `curl -sS "$HTTPS_PROXY/__agentproxy/status"`.

Consequences:

- **547 distinct external URLs unchecked** - 225 Amazon/`amzn.to` affiliate links, 16 nps.gov,
  69 YouTube embeds, the remainder agency/reservation portals and reference citations. Up from
  469 last cycle, so the 16 new guides added 78 unverifiable outbound links.
  **Nothing was edited or removed on the strength of a failed request.**
- **257 distinct image URLs unchecked** - 115 `m.media-amazon.com`, 112 Unsplash, 30 Wikimedia.
  Up from 227. **No image URL was changed**, and no photo ID was invented.
- **`npm run validate:affiliate` exits 1 reporting "130 broken, 0 OK".** Note the failure text
  differs from the brief's description: this run emits `[error: fetch failed]` rather than
  `[403]`, and **zero** `[403]` lines. Same cause, different surface - a run where 0 of 130
  links succeed is a transport failure, not 130 dead links. **This says nothing about
  affiliate-link health in either direction.** Do not read it as broken; do not read it as
  verified. The brief's description of this known-noise item should be updated to cover both
  failure strings, or a future run may mistake it for a real regression.

To restore these three checks, the environment's network policy needs `nps.gov`,
`recreation.gov`, the state park agency domains, `images.unsplash.com`, `amazon.com`,
`amzn.to` and `m.media-amazon.com` allow-listed. Until then this audit is repo-only plus
WebSearch.
