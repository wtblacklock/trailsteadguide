## 2. Fixed

### Factual correction (1 guide, 1 place)

**Acadia's reservation window was stated as 60 days in the one place it mattered most.**
`app/guides/camping-in-the-northeast-for-beginners/page.tsx:247`, in the common-mistakes list,
read:

> Blackwoods and Seawall release 60 days ahead and book in seconds; NH state parks open 11 months out.

No part of that first clause is right, and the same file already said so four times over (lines
132, 188, 335, and the `faqPageGraph` entry at line 67, all "6 months out"). Corroborated from
two independent directions before editing:

- 90% of Blackwoods and Seawall sites are released **six months ahead, on the first of each
  month, at 10 a.m. ET**, with the remaining 10% released **14 days** before arrival on a rolling
  basis ([Acadia camping guide](https://thedyrt.com/guides/np/acadia-national-park-camping-guide);
  [Blackwoods Campground reference](https://npplan.com/parks-by-state/maine-national-parks/acadia-national-park-park-at-a-glance/acadia-national-park-camping/acadia-national-park-blackwoods-campground/)).
- Recreation.gov's standard is the 6-month rolling window, with per-facility exceptions including
  short 14/30/90-day windows ([Recreation.gov booking windows](https://outdoorithm.com/booking/recreation-gov)).

Now reads: *"Blackwoods and Seawall release most sites six months ahead, on the first of the
month, and book in seconds; only a small 14-day release is left by then."*

The fix adds specificity rather than retreating from it, and it makes the surrounding paragraph
work for the first time: the mistake being described is "trying to book two months out", and the
14-day second release is precisely what a reader at that point still has. No QuickAnswer, FAQ or
JSON-LD change was needed - those four were already correct, so page and structured data now
agree instead of disagreeing.

## 3. Flagged, not fixed

### Seasonal is being routed around - now a three-baseline pattern

This is the clearest signal in the catalogue, and the mid-PR commits made it much stronger. Of
the eight guides that landed this cycle, seven are plainly season-shaped and **none** was filed
as seasonal:

| Guide | Season-shaped because | Filed as |
|---|---|---|
| `camping-after-dark-with-kids` | fall's shrinking daylight | `scenario` |
| `camping-in-bear-country-with-kids` | fall being peak bear activity | `scenario` |
| `how-to-keep-kids-warm-camping` | cold nights | `scenario` |
| `setting-up-camp-in-the-dark` | arriving after an early sunset | `basics` |
| `cold-weather-camping-meals` | cold weather | `basics` |
| `tent-heater-safety` | cold-weather heating | `scenario` |
| `columbus-day-weekend-camping` | a specific holiday weekend | `scenario` |
| `how-to-prevent-tent-condensation` | arguable - worse in cold, but year-round | `basics` |

**The decisive one is `columbus-day-weekend-camping`.** Holiday-weekend camping is listed in
`content-gap-analysis.md` as a Tier 3 *seasonal* gap. The guide is a direct sibling to
`labor-day-weekend-camping`. It shipped as `scenario` - exactly as Labor Day and Halloween did
before it. When the thing the backlog calls a seasonal gap gets built and filed as a scenario,
the category label is no longer doing any work.

Counting back to the 2026-09-03 baseline, **ten guides whose entire premise is a season or a
holiday have been filed under basics or scenario**: off-season gear storage, Halloween, Labor
Day, Columbus Day, after dark with kids, bear country, keeping kids warm, cold-weather meals,
tent heater safety, and setting up camp in the dark. Seasonal has sat at exactly 4 since the May
snapshot while the catalogue went 35 to 66; it is now 6.1% of the guide surface.

So the honest framing is no longer "seasonal is neglected" but **"seasonal is a dead category
that four old guides happen to sit in"**, and the open question is whether to revive it or
retire it. Recategorising the ten above would take it from 4 to 14 with no new writing;
retiring it and folding the four into basics/scenario is equally defensible. Both are taxonomy
calls with URL, sitemap and internal-link consequences, so nothing was recategorised here.

A related observation: those guides form an unplanned, coherent cluster -
**camping with kids in adverse fall conditions** (dark, cold, bears, heaters, after-dark
arrival) - that is six guides deep with no hub or browse surface, and `relatedGuides` is doing
that work by hand.

## 6. Gap recommendations

Ranked. Item 1 is unchanged from the last two audits and the case for it is now materially
stronger.

1. **Build the canonical reservation-systems reference table.** *(S effort, high lift)* Three
   consecutive audits have now fixed booking-window errors caused by 19 guides independently
   restating the same external facts. The progression is the argument: 2026-09-03 found three
   mutually inconsistent Recreation.gov release times across three guides; 2026-09-07 found
   California generalising Yosemite's exception to six other parks and Utah citing a nonexistent
   12-month window; this week found a **single guide disagreeing with itself in five places.**
   Per-guide accuracy is not improving, and there are now 66 guides. One sourced table (state,
   system, URL, window, release time) that guides link to instead of restating is both a citable
   asset and the structural fix. **Ship this before the next state guide.**
2. **Add a CI check for the metadata limits.** *(XS effort, high lift)* New this week, and the
   cheapest high-value item on this list. Over-length `metaTitle`/`metaDescription` is the single
   most frequent finding across all three audits, and this cycle it arrived faster than the audit
   could clear it: **18 violations in 16 commits**, including all five new guides, a new compare
   page, and both guides touched by an SEO refresh pass whose entire purpose was metadata. A
   weekly human audit is the wrong instrument for a two-integer constraint that is already
   documented in `lib/guides/types.ts`. A script walking `GUIDES` plus the page-level
   `META_TITLE`/`DESCRIPTION` constants, wired into the existing `Lint (fast feedback)` job,
   would end the category permanently and take less time than this week's sweep did. It also
   catches the `data.ts`-vs-page drift that bit three guides this cycle.
3. **Guide: "What NOT to bring camping".** *(backlog Tier 1 #6, S effort)* Still the only
   unshipped Tier 1 content item and still the cheapest remaining win. Unchanged for three audits,
   which is itself worth noticing.
4. **Decide what `seasonal` is for: revive it or retire it.** *(S effort, editorial)* Ten
   season-or-holiday guides across three baselines have all been filed elsewhere, including the
   Columbus Day guide that the backlog itself lists as a seasonal gap. Either recategorise (those
   ten would take the category 4 to 14 with no new writing) or retire the label and fold the four
   broad-season guides into basics/scenario. Both are defensible; the current state is the worst
   of both, and it has now persisted long enough to be a decision rather than an oversight.
5. **Hub for the "kids in adverse conditions" cluster.** *(S effort)* New this week, and it grew
   mid-audit. After dark, bear country, keeping kids warm, cold-weather meals, tent heater safety
   and setting up camp in the dark are **six** guides deep with no browse surface, and the cluster
   is the most coherent thing to ship in the catalogue right now. Cheaper than the location index
   and with a clearer audience.
6. **Second wave of state guides: WA, OR, TN (NC already in flight).** *(M effort each)* Region
   guides already link these agencies without a destination. **North Carolina is open in PR #120
   as of this writing**, so treat it as taken. Do the rest *after* item 1, so the new guides
   consume the table rather than adding more copies of the same facts - and note that the NC PR
   predates the table, so it is the next candidate to be retrofitted onto it.
7. **Gear bundles to match the catalogue.** *(backlog Tier 2 #12, S-M effort)* **115** products
   against 4 bundles, after 15 landed mid-audit. The gap widens every week. Budget and mid tiers
   are the natural cuts.
8. **Location index / state picker.** *(S effort)* 18 location guides with no browse surface between
   the hub and the article.
9. **Upgrade the New York booking window** to the specific NYSDEC 9-month window - see flagged. A
   natural by-product of item 1.

Editorial items deliberately kept out of the commit: all new content above, the seasonal
recategorisation, the New York upgrade, the Florida non-resident caveat, the `plan-templates`
range, and any image replacement.
