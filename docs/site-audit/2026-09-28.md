## 2. Fixed

### Factual correction: Acadia's reservation cadence (1 guide, 4 places)

`app/guides/camping-in-the-northeast-for-beginners/page.tsx` contradicted itself. The body
paragraph and both FAQ copies said "recreation.gov, 6 months out"; the common-mistakes list said
"Blackwoods and Seawall release 60 days ahead and book in seconds". 60 days is not how Acadia
works and never has been.

Corroborated from two independent directions before editing:

- The NPS Acadia news release on campground reservation availability
  ([nps.gov/acad](https://home.nps.gov/acad/learn/news/20241125.htm)) states that 90 percent of
  Blackwoods and Seawall sites are released six months in advance on the **first of each month at
  10am ET**, with the remaining 10 percent released at 10am on a rolling **14 days** ahead of
  arrival.
- The same rule is reported independently by National Park Magazine and the Bar Harbor Story
  coverage of that release, with the worked example (arrive May 24, book Dec 1 or May 10).

So "6 months out" was directionally right but described the wrong mechanism - it implied the
standard rolling daily drop, when Acadia is a block release. The "60 days ahead" claim was simply
wrong, and it was in the one place a reader would act on it (the mistakes list, whose entire
point is "do not show up two months out").

Corrected in four places, each keeping the specific numbers rather than retreating to vaguer
language:

- the common-mistakes item (now names the 90/10 split, the 1st-of-the-month 10am ET block, and the
  14-day tail),
- the "National parks and federal lands" body paragraph (6 months out, block on the 1st rather
  than the usual rolling daily schedule),
- the `faqPageGraph` JSON-LD answer,
- the matching visible FAQ answer, so page and structured data agree.

Cross-checked against `recreation-gov-reservation-strategy`, which already files Acadia in its
"5-6 months out, on the day the window opens" row. That table was correct and is unchanged; the
northeast guide now agrees with it.

### The dash grep in the brief still silently fails in this sandbox

Confirmed again, and the previous audit's workaround is still the right one. `grep -P
'\x{2014}|\x{2013}'` errors with `character code point value in \x{} or \o{} is too large` under
this container's non-UTF-8 locale, and with stderr redirected it produces empty output that looks
identical to a clean result. This audit used a Python codepoint scan over `app/`, `components/`,
`lib/`, `data/` instead, which is what the section 5 result is based on. **Recommend the brief be
updated** to specify the Python scan or a `LC_ALL=C.UTF-8` prefix - this is the second audit in a
row to have to rediscover it.

## 4. Blocked by sandbox

**Blocked is not clean.** No claim is made in either direction about anything in this section.

Every outbound host this audit needs is refused by the session's egress proxy. Confirmed this run
by direct probe: `www.nps.gov`, `www.recreation.gov`, `images.unsplash.com` and `www.amazon.com`
all return curl code `000`, and the proxy reported all four back as
`connect_rejected (the egress proxy denied the CONNECT (organization policy))`. `WebFetch`
returns `EGRESS_BLOCKED` for the same hosts, so it is not a fallback. `WebSearch` works and was
the sole research tool for section 7.

Consequences:

- **414 distinct external URLs unchecked** - 65 agency/reference links across 52 hosts (NPS,
  recreation.gov, 30+ state park systems and reservation portals), 156 Amazon / `amzn.to`
  affiliate links, 44 YouTube embeds, 5 Reddit citations. **Nothing was edited or removed on the
  strength of a failed request.**
- **207 distinct image URLs unchecked** - 93 Unsplash, 30 Wikimedia, the rest Amazon product
  images and local assets. **No image URL was changed**, and no photo ID was invented.
- **`npm run validate:affiliate` exits 1 with every link reported `[403]`.** These are proxy
  CONNECT denials, not Amazon responses - the script issues a request and reports the proxy's
  refusal as if it were the origin's status code. It cannot tell the two apart, so **this run says
  nothing about affiliate-link health in either direction.** Do not read it as broken; do not read
  it as verified.

To restore these three checks, the environment's network policy needs `nps.gov`, `recreation.gov`,
the state park agency domains, `images.unsplash.com` and `amazon.com` allow-listed. Until then
this audit is repo-only plus WebSearch, and that has now been true for three consecutive runs.
