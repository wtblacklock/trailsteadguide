# Site audit - 2026-09-21

Weekly content-gap and site-health audit. Run against `main` at `67bfbea`.

**Headline: the audit pipeline is working and the merge pipeline is not.**

This run found and fixed 5 mechanical defects, all of them in the two guides that landed this
week. That is the normal, healthy result. The abnormal result is what it found around them:

- **PR #93 (the 2026-09-14 audit) is still open and unmerged**, one week later. Everything it
  fixed is still wrong on production right now. That includes three verified factual errors
  (Acadia's booking window, a wrong Recreation.gov release time on the Labor Day guide, and a
  Florida window that predates HB 109) and 17 over-length SEO titles across every `/compare`
  page. All three factual defects were re-confirmed present on `main` this run.
- **15 pull requests are open**, the oldest from 2026-09-03. Two of them are weekly audits.
  Nothing has merged since #99 on 2026-09-17.

So the top finding this week is not a content defect. It is that three weeks of audit output is
queued behind review, and the site is serving text that two consecutive audits have already
proven wrong. **The highest-value action available is merging #93, not reading this report.**

Consequently this audit **deliberately does not re-fix anything PR #93 already fixes.** Doing so
would put two branches on the same lines and hand the reviewer a merge conflict on work that is
already done and already sourced. Those 20 items are listed in section 3 as pending-in-#93, not
re-flagged as new.

External network checks remain blocked by the session's egress policy, for the fourth consecutive
run. Dead-link, dead-image and affiliate-link health are **unverified, not clean**.

## 2. Fixed

All five values are in the two guides added since the last audit. This is the fourth consecutive
audit where **every** metadata defect found was in that week's new content, which is now a strong
enough pattern to act on - see recommendation 2.

### `how-to-keep-kids-warm-camping`

| Field | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| `metaTitle` / `META_TITLE` | 44 | **38** - `How to Keep Kids Warm Camping at Night` |
| `metaDescription` / `DESCRIPTION` | 157 | **153** |

On the title: the budget could not hold `How to Keep Kids Warm Camping on Cold Nights` (44). Per
the standing rule from the printables-title incident, the cut preserves the head query
`How to Keep Kids Warm Camping` verbatim and replaces only the trailing qualifier
`on Cold Nights` with `at Night`. "Warm" already carries the cold implication, so the dropped word
is the redundant one; dropping `How to` instead would have saved more characters but thrown away
the query form the page is actually written against. The page `H1` (`TITLE`) is unchanged.

On the description: dropped a single article - `the mistakes that make kids cold` becomes
`mistakes that make kids cold`. Nothing else changed; every listed element survives.

### `camping-in-bear-country-with-kids`

| Field | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| `metaDescription` (data.ts) | 169 | **153** |
| `DESCRIPTION` (page constant) | 190 | **153** |

These two were **different strings** as well as both over budget, so the page and the catalogue
were describing the same guide two different ways. Both now carry the same trimmed text:

> Camping in bear country with kids: why fall is peak bear season, what goes in the bear box,
> three rules kids can remember, and what to do if you see one.

The wording is the repo's own (it is the data.ts phrasing, trimmed), not new copy. `the busiest
bear season` becomes `peak bear season` and `what actually goes in` becomes `what goes in`; the
four content promises are unchanged. Verified against the article body that "bear box" is
accurate and central - it is the guide's core instruction, appearing in the QuickAnswer tldr, the
FAQ answer, and its own `<h3>`.

## 4. Blocked by sandbox

**Blocked is not clean.** No claim is made in either direction about anything in this section.

Confirmed by direct probe this run: `www.nps.gov`, `www.recreation.gov`, `images.unsplash.com` and
`www.amazon.com` all return curl code `000`, and all four appear in the proxy's own
`recentRelayFailures` log as `connect_rejected` /
`gateway answered 403 to CONNECT (policy denial or upstream failure)`. `WebFetch` returns
`EGRESS_BLOCKED` for the same hosts, so it is not a fallback. `WebSearch` works and was the sole
research tool for section 7.

Consequences:

- **414 distinct external URLs across 69 hosts unchecked** - 156 Amazon/`amzn.to`, 44 YouTube
  embeds, 11 nps.gov, the rest state park and agency domains, Reddit citations, schema.org and
  test fixtures. **Nothing was edited or removed on the strength of a failed request.**
- **205 distinct image URLs unchecked** - 93 Unsplash, 78 `m.media-amazon.com`, 30 Wikimedia, 4
  `images-na.ssl-images-amazon.com`. **No image URL was changed**, and no photo ID was invented.
  Both counts are up from 09-14 (203 images, 406 URLs), consistent with two new guides landing.
- **`npm run validate:affiliate` is unusable.** It exits 1 with **160 `[403]` lines and nothing
  else** - every link checked, no other status code anywhere in the output (up from 156 last week,
  consistent with new gear landing). These
  are proxy CONNECT denials, not Amazon responses. The script cannot tell the two apart, so
  **this run says nothing about affiliate-link health in either direction.** Do not read it as
  broken; do not read it as verified.

Partial mitigation, unchanged in kind from last week: every agency domain the site links to
appeared as a live, current URL in this run's WebSearch results - `nps.gov`, `recreation.gov`,
`dnr.wisconsin.gov`, `michigan.gov/dnr`, `midnrreservations.com`, `floridastateparks.org`,
`reserve.floridastateparks.org`. That is evidence against a silent domain rename. It is **not**
evidence that any individual deep link still resolves.

To restore these three checks, the environment's network policy needs `nps.gov`, `recreation.gov`,
the state park agency domains, `images.unsplash.com` and `amazon.com` allow-listed. Until then
this audit is repo-only plus WebSearch. **This is the fourth consecutive audit reporting the same
three checks as unverifiable**; if the allow-list is not going to change, that is worth deciding
explicitly rather than re-reporting weekly.

## 6. Gap recommendations

Ranked. Item 1 is new this week and outranks the content items because it blocks them.

1. **Merge the queue, starting with #93.** *(no effort, highest lift)* Three verified factual
   errors and 17 over-length titles are fixed, sourced and waiting. Every week they sit, the site
   serves text two audits have proven wrong and the next audit spends its budget rediscovering
   them. If the queue is stuck because 15 PRs is too much to review at once, the audit PRs are the
   cheapest to clear: they are small, mechanical, and their reasoning is written out in
   `docs/site-audit/`. **Merge #93 before reading the rest of this list.**
2. **Enforce the metadata budgets in the type system or a test.** *(S effort, high lift)* Promoted
   from #2 last week on new evidence: this is now the fourth consecutive audit where every single
   metadata defect found was in that week's new content, and the count is not falling (8, then 17,
   then 5). The weekly audit has become the metadata linter, on a one-week delay. A vitest case
   that walks the catalogue and asserts the budgets is cheap, would have caught all 5 of this
   week's fixes and all 17 of #93's, and would move the check from "found next Monday" to "found
   before the PR opens". **This is the item that retires a recurring audit task.**
3. **Build the canonical reservation-systems reference table.** *(S effort, high lift)* Fourth
   consecutive audit carrying this. No new drift this week only because neither new guide asserts
   a booking window - which is the argument, not a reprieve. Six content guides are queued in the
   merge backlog and will land as six more independent restatements of the same external facts.
   **Ship this before those merge, not after.**
4. **Decide what `seasonal` is for.** *(S effort, editorial)* Fourth audit raising it, now with a
   sharper framing: the problem is not that seasonal is starved, it is that **scenario is the
   default bucket** at 19 of 61. Either recategorise (after-dark, gear storage, Halloween and
   keep-kids-warm are all defensible seasonal entries, taking the category from 4 to 8 with no new

5. **Split `metaDescription` out of `Printable`** (and consider the same for `Skill`).
   *(S effort, medium lift)* Unblocks 16 of the 21 flagged dynamic-route violations at once.
   `Guide` already separates the two. Naturally paired with item 2.
6. **Guide: "What NOT to bring camping".** *(backlog Tier 1 #6, S effort)* Still the only unshipped
   Tier 1 content item, four weeks running. Cheapest remaining content win.
7. **Second wave of state guides: WA, OR, NC, TN.** *(M effort each)* After items 2 and 3, so the
   new guides consume the table and the metadata check.
8. **Gear bundles to match the catalogue.** *(backlog Tier 2 #12, S-M effort)* 100 products against
   4 bundles. Budget and mid tiers are the natural cuts.
9. **Location index / state picker.** *(S effort)* 18 location guides with no browse surface.
10. **Upgrade the New York booking window** to the specific NYSDEC 9-month window and release
